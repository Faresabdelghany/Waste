# Backend architecture

Status: agreed 2026-09-17. This repository is the monorepo of a new system built from scratch: it began as the system's frontend, now `apps/web`, and the backend described here is built beside it, `apps/api` first. This document describes that backend. The five invariants and the optimiser choice are recorded as ADRs under `../adr/`; stack choices and their rejected alternatives live in the table below. This document is the readable whole and is updated when an ADR changes it. Terms follow `CONTEXT.md`.

## Bounded contexts

Derived from the glossary. Each context owns its tables and exposes commands and read models; other contexts reference its records by id only.

| Context | Owns | Defining trait |
|---|---|---|
| Organisation & Access | Company, Project, User account, Role and its grants, Project Access, Service Provider (the external organisation itself: legal name, registration number, country, contact), Service Provider Access, Company Administrator | The tenant is the Company. A Service Provider's users belong to another organisation and see only their currently assigned Service Areas. |
| Registry | Customer, Property, Property Group, Shared Collection Point, Agreement, Subscription, Service frequency, Product / Service, Container Service Placement | Effective-dated entitlements. Service frequency is inherited by query, never copied. |
| Resources | Vehicle Type and its container-type compatibility, Vehicle (with compartments), Driver, Vehicle Allocation, Warehouse, Depot, Unloading Station, Stock Movement, Container Asset State | The Stock Movement ledger is append-only — rows are inserted and never updated or deleted, the API role's `UPDATE` and `DELETE` revoked — and Container Asset State is a query over it, the latest movement of a container folded onto one of four states, never a table. A Vehicle Allocation is one current-reservation row under exclusion constraints over its window, and every action on it is an appended event: the row is what the database holds, the events the ledger nobody rewrites. |
| Planning | Collection Calendar, Route Scheme, Collection Group, Stop Matching Rule, Planning Area, Scenario, Plan | Stores rules and immutable optimisation results (Plans), never generated work. Generation is a job. A Route in Execution references at most one active Plan. |
| Execution | Route, Planned and Actual Assignment, Pickup, Session, Proof of Service, Unload, the receipt of every driver command, the outbox | Connectivity can disappear at any point in a shift. Pickup is one record from planning through execution; Proof of Service and Unload are append-only ledgers referencing the Route or the Pickup, the API role's `UPDATE` and `DELETE` revoked; the receipt (`driver_command`) is a third ledger keyed by the device's own id — the idempotency table of ADR-0004, the place a rejected command is kept, and what syncs back down — and the outbox is the one table other contexts hear from, written in the request's transaction after the rows it describes. |
| Resolution | Ticket and its appended history, Alert | The Ticket is the case that owns what has to be dealt with — a request, a deviation, a complaint, a task, an operational issue — opened by the office and, through the outbox, from an execution event (a failed or unattended stop, a reported problem, a rejected command), and moved only by commands, each of which appends one row to its history, `ticket_event`, an append-only ledger of kinds with shapes (status changes, assignments, comments with their visibility), so the case's row is a snapshot the ledger repeats. The Alert is a condition acknowledged and resolved, current state with no ledger, linked to at most one Ticket. Resolution writes no row of another context — a re-collection is a Route the ticket names — and publishes its own news (`ticket-opened`, `ticket-completed`, `ticket-rejected`) into the one outbox, where Finance reads it. |
| Finance & Contracting | Price List and its rows, Service Area with its planning areas and waste fractions, Service Area Assignment, Service Provider Price, Billable Event, Billing Run and its exclusions, Invoice and its lines (a credit note is an Invoice of `kind = credit-note`), Settlement with its lines and its history, Weight Review | A pipeline from Pickup to Invoice, and from Billable Event to Settlement. A list, a row, an area, an assignment, a price and a settlement are effective-dated `validity` rows under an exclusion constraint each — one list of a code, one row of a product and a condition set (keyed on a generated column), one area of a code, one holder of an area, one price of an assignment and a product, one settlement of an assignment over a period — and a price change or an indexation is a new row, never an update. A Billable Event is current state whose status is a reading over its stamps and the invoice line naming it; an Invoice, its lines, a run's exclusions, a settlement's history and a weight review are ledgers, appended and never rewritten, so a correction is a further document: a credit note, a reversal event, a new Unload row. Money is an integer in minor units. A Service Provider's areas, assignments, prices and settlements are here and are the first office rows its account reaches, through the assignment valid on a day; the organisation record itself is Organisation & Access's, because an access grant needs the organisation it grants for. Weight control is the review of an Unload — approved, rejected, or corrected by a new Unload row — over Execution's rows without changing one. Finance consumes the outbox (`pickup-completed`, `pickup-corrected`, `ticket-completed`) and publishes `invoice-issued` and `settlement-closed` for the e-conomic export. |

Intelligence (Insight, Suggestion, Approval) and Promotion / Go-live are glossary terms with no schema in the first version. Activating a Plan on a Route (ADR-0002) is not Promotion; Promotion is the larger process of turning an approved plan into production configuration.

## Invariants

These are architectural constraints, not implementation preferences. Each has an ADR.

1. **Single domain data path.** Domain tables are not reachable from the browser. The Hono API is the web boundary; authorisation and business rules live behind it. Row-level security is a backstop, never the place the model is implemented. ADR-0001.
2. **Generation is not optimisation.** Generation deterministically establishes what work exists on a service date. Optimisation produces a Plan over that work and never rewrites the generated baseline. ADR-0002.
3. **Container lifecycle consistency.** One Container identity, a Service Placement in Registry, an Asset State projected from the Stock Movement ledger, and lifecycle commands that update both contexts atomically. ADR-0003.
4. **Offline execution.** Every driver action is an idempotent, client-identified command that survives hours without connectivity and is applied server-authoritatively later. ADR-0004.
5. **Effective dating.** Every effective-dated concept uses validity periods with database-enforced non-overlap, evaluated as of a date at query time. ADR-0005.

## Stack

| Layer | Choice | Why this and not the obvious alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Web | Next.js 16, React 19, TypeScript, shadcn/ui, Tailwind v4 | Already built. |
| API | Hono with hono-openapi and zod 4 | Zod schemas shared with the web forms. OpenAPI 3.1 is the contract for the driver app and third parties. tRPC has no first-party OpenAPI. ts-rest is unmaintained. Server Actions are Next-only. |
| Database | Supabase Postgres 17 with PostGIS, Stockholm (`eu-north-1`) | PostGIS for container points, planning-area and service-area polygons, route geometry. Supabase chosen as platform; Neon was the alternative. |
| Auth | Supabase Auth, tokens verified with jose | Its JWT is consumed natively by PowerSync and RLS. It has no organisation model, so Company, Project Access, Service Provider Access, and roles are our tables. The only claim of ours on the JWT is the home company id, which the custom access token hook adds; the user id is Auth's own subject, and grants are looked up per request. The API verifies an access token against the project's published JWKS (asymmetric keys, ES256 on `waste-dev`) through jose's cached remote key set, so it holds no shared secret and key rotation needs no restart; Hono's own JWK middleware refetches the key set on every request. Better Auth was the alternative and is the fallback if the platform changes. |
| ORM | Drizzle 0.45 | Typed SQL, transactions for multi-table commands. Geometry as GeoJSON through a custom type that drizzle-kit renders as written (`geometry(Point, 4326)`; Drizzle's own geometry column is not used, since drizzle-kit rewrites its polygon form to point). Hand-written SQL only for what Drizzle has no builder for: extensions, functions, roles, exclusion constraints, policies. Prisma rejected: PostGIS columns are unsupported in its client. |
| Jobs | pg-boss 12 | Same Postgres, same transaction as the rows it produces, cron built in. Needs the direct connection or session pooler, never the transaction pooler. Worker hosted outside Supabase, on Fly.io beside the API (ADR-0007). |
| Cross-context events | Outbox table relayed by the worker | No message broker. |
| Driver app | Expo with PowerSync | The only sync engine with offline writes, server-authoritative conflict handling, and an attachment queue on any Postgres. Zero rejects offline writes; Replicache is in maintenance mode. PowerSync's service is source-available under FSL: self-host or EU cloud. Fallback: plain Expo SQLite outbox. |
| Road routing | Valhalla, self-hosted on Fly.io with a volume for the tiles (ADR-0007) | Truck costing, distance matrices, map-matching, MIT. Denmark extract is about 470 MB. The public OSRM demo server is non-commercial, one request per second, no uptime guarantee: prototype only. |
| Optimisation | PyVRP 0.14 as a Python sidecar behind one HTTP contract (ADR-0006) | Multi-dimensional capacity plus reloads in one model, verified by spike. Its reload empties every compartment, which matches Danish and Swedish practice. The only non-TypeScript component; OR-Tools replaces it behind the same contract if partial unloading is ever required. ADR-0006. |
| Map data | GeoJSON with client clustering; Martin vector tiles above roughly 50k features | Martin is maintained by the MapLibre organisation. pg_tileserv has stalled. |

## Data model rules

- Domain tables live in schema `wms`, which is not in Supabase's exposed schemas. RLS policies on them are coarse tenant fences; the API sets request claims per transaction so the backstop is real.
- The API connects as `wms_api`, which owns nothing and bypasses nothing, so every policy and default privilege applies to it; migrations run as the owner.
- Every table carries `company_id`; project-scoped tables carry `project_id`. Service Provider visibility is resolved through Service Area Assignment validity, never denormalised onto rows.
- An intra-tenant reference is a composite foreign key that carries the tenant: `(company_id, project_id) → project (company_id, id)`, `(company_id, role_id) → role (company_id, id)`, and so on, so every table referenced this way carries `unique (company_id, id)` for the key to point at. Only `company_id → company (id)` itself is a plain key, and `company` is its own tenant (`company_id = id`). A row therefore cannot reference another tenant's record whatever the API does; the database refuses it.
- A project-scoped reference carries the project. Where both tables are project-scoped the key carries `project_id` as well: `(company_id, project_id, property_id) → property (company_id, project_id, id)`, so a subscription cannot name a property of another project any more than of another company, and a placement cannot name another project's container. The referenced table carries `unique (company_id, project_id, id)` for it. A reference from a project-scoped table to a company-wide one — an agreement's customer, a placement's waste fraction — stays the tenant's composite key, because the target has no project to agree on. The helpers are `projectReference` and `projectKey` beside `tenantReference` and `tenantKey`.
- A record that hangs off another names it once and reaches the rest through it. ADR-0003 has a Container Service Placement name the place, the agreement, the product and the waste fraction; it names the waste fraction directly and reaches the other three through its Subscription, one reference in place of three that could disagree with each other.
- Supabase Auth is the identity provider and sign-up is invitation-only. The custom access token hook adds the one custom claim, `app_metadata.company_id`, the caller's home company; the API verifies the token against the project's JWKS and looks the caller's account and grants up on every request inside `withCompany`, so a revocation takes effect on the next call and RLS stays the backstop.
- Effective-dated tables use `valid_from` and nullable `valid_to`, half-open, with a `btree_gist` exclusion constraint per key.
- A status is stored only where validity does not already say it. "Pending", "expiring", "expired" and "terminated" are readings of a period against the day the caller names, never columns; a record with no period — a Customer, a Property, a Product — carries a status instead, and an effective-dated one carries a status only for what its period cannot say, as `agreement.status` (draft, active, cancelled) does. A status gates a new reference and never an existing one: an agreement's customer and payer must be active, a subscription's product active and its place served (a property active, a point open or restricted), and a container is not placed at a property that has gone inactive or a point that has closed — each a 409 the API holds, spelled once per family — while the agreements, subscriptions and placements already made stand whatever their rows do afterwards, a row's own status never blocks a write to that row, and a project's, a group's and an agreement's status stay informational (an onboarding project is one being set up, a group is built while draft, an agreement's period says the stronger thing). A record's own key may be held by the exclusion constraint rather than by a unique one where the stronger thing is true: one Agreement of a number is valid at a time, so the number may name a later Agreement once the earlier has ended.
- One spelling of a vocabulary: a context's closed lists — its kinds, statuses, roles, purposes, modes and units — are spelled once in the domain package (`@waste/domain/registry/vocabulary` for the Registry, `@waste/domain/planning/vocabulary` for Planning, `@waste/domain/resources/vocabulary` for Resources, `@waste/domain/execution/vocabulary` for Execution, `@waste/domain/resolution/vocabulary` for Resolution, `@waste/domain/finance/vocabulary` for Finance & Contracting) and read from there by the column's `CHECK ... in (...)` and by the wire's `z.enum`, so the check at the API boundary and the check in the table cannot drift and there is nothing to hold in lockstep. Adding a value is still a code change and a migration: the table's check names its values. (The Organisation & Access statuses came first and keep their two spellings and their lockstep test.)
- A closed vocabulary that a row holds several of — the days a Route Scheme serves, the days a Collection Group runs on, the days a Project rests on — is a `text[]` under a subset check, `CHECK (column <@ ARRAY['…']::text[])` over the same tuple, with a separate non-empty check where an empty set would mean a scheme that never runs; never a child table, since a weekday is a token and not a row anyone references. The database holds subset-ness and non-emptiness; distinctness and the size of the set are the contracts' rule, since the API is the only writer. Where a bound is the table's own rule — a project's weekend leaves it a working day — it is an inline check beside the subset check.
- A versioned geometry is two tables: an identity that other records name (`planning_area`, by its stable `code`) and an effective-dated version table that holds the polygon (`planning_area_boundary`), one version in force at a time under the exclusion constraint. A record that named a version would break the day a new one started, and the version chain is the identity plus non-overlapping periods, which the constraint holds where a `previous_version_id` could not.
- The ring rule: a polygon reaches the API as a closed flat ring — the contracts' `FlatPolygon`, an outer ring of four or more `[lng, lat]` positions closing on the first with three distinct, holes allowed, no winding rule and no altitude, since the column is flat — closed by the client and never repaired at the boundary, which refuses rather than repairs everywhere else. PostGIS's `st_isvalid` in the column's check is the second stop, and its refusal of a ring that crosses itself is answered as a 400 on the field (`refuseCheck`), because the row is alone and its own value will not do, which is what a schema says of a value it refuses; the only difference is who noticed.
- A set that travels with a record is read with the record and replaced whole, never diffed, the record's own row stamped first so `updatedAt` moves and the replacement delete-then-insert inside the request's transaction: a Property's parties and a Group's or Point's members through one mechanism with a descriptor per family (`routes/members.ts`), a Collection Calendar's holidays through a sibling spelled in its route, since an entry there is a row named by id with a role and a holiday is a day with a name and names no row, and a Collection Group's Stop Matching Rule and picked containers through a sibling of their own (`replaceGroupSet`), since the rule is three things over two tables and a column and the list is positioned, 1..n in the body's order. A set of ids and nothing else on a company-wide record — a Vehicle Type's container types, an Unloading Station's fractions — goes through the id-only sibling (`routes/id-sets.ts`), and a Vehicle's compartments, each a row with fraction rows of its own, through one of their own; the two statements every set runs, a page's entries grouped by parent and a body's ids held to what their key allows, are spelled once (`routes/sets.ts`). A create may carry the set it starts with; a patch never touches it, because a patch is a field-by-field change and a set is a set.
- Generated Routes are unique on route scheme, collection group, and service date. Generation is idempotent.
- Pickup is a current-state row. Proof of Service and Unload are append-only rows referencing the Route or Pickup.
- A ledger is appended and never rewritten. A table whose rows are never updated — the Stock Movement ledger, the Vehicle Allocation events — spreads one `recorded_at` in place of `created_at` and `updated_at`, carries no `updated_at` trigger, and its migration file revokes `UPDATE` and `DELETE` from the API role (`REVOKE UPDATE, DELETE … FROM wms_api`, spelled by `appendOnly`), so a row nobody can change through the API is still a row the owner can remove by hand, for a test or an erasure. What a ledger says about the present is a query, never a table of its own: Container Asset State is `assetStateOf`, the latest Stock Movement of a container in recording order — by `id`, a UUIDv7, and not by `occurred_at`, since a command holds every movement's `from` to where the previous one arrived under the container's row lock, so the chain is consistent only in the order it was written and a back-dated row must not reorder it — folded onto one of the glossary's four states, read beside every Container and stored nowhere. The invariant the ledger needs, a container in one place at a time and every movement leaving from where it is, is the API's under the container's row lock; Postgres's shape checks on a movement's kind and places are the backstop.
- A reservation is a bounded window on a clock under a partial exclusion constraint. A Vehicle Allocation reserves a vehicle from an instant to an instant (`planned_from`, `planned_to`, both `timestamptz` and both given: two routes a day on one vehicle is ordinary, and a reservation without an end is not a plan), and the database holds one live reservation of a vehicle, of a driver and of a trailer at a time through three `btree_gist` exclusion constraints over `tstzrange(planned_from, planned_to, '[)')`, each with a predicate that leaves released rows and null keys out of the index (`excludeOverlappingWindow`). That is ADR-0005's invariant — database-enforced non-overlap — kept over instants where the effective-dated tables keep it over days, and the ADR does not change. The row is the current reservation, updated by four commands and by nothing else, and every command appends an event carrying the snapshot the allocation then had, so the history is complete by construction and there is no PATCH.
- The issue command owns the placement create. `POST /containers/:id/placements` is ADR-0003's "issue into service" and the only door into service: it takes the container's row lock, reads its asset state, refuses a container that is not in stock, writes the Registry's Container Service Placement and appends the `issue` movement in one transaction, both rows or neither. A placement's end is the ledger's: the create carries no `validTo`, `return` and `decommission` end the placement and append the movement together, and the placement's patch corrects an end already set and never opens or closes one — a null end is refused at the boundary, an end on an open placement by the route. A placement starts inside its subscription's period and, open, may outlive a bounded subscription on paper until the container comes back, since the person issuing it cannot say when; the return's end is held inside the period, and a subscription is not shortened under an open placement.
- A stored point is flat. Every point a column holds — a property's, a shared collection point's, a warehouse's, a depot's, an unloading station's — reaches the API as the contracts' `FlatPoint`, `[longitude, latitude]` and nothing else, so a third ordinate is a 400 at the coordinates and never PostGIS's 22023 after the write; `Point`, with its optional altitude, stays for a geometry no column holds.
- A column is dropped behind a guard that proves it empty, or behind a backfill that carries its values across, never bare. The first `DROP COLUMN` (`collection_group.rule_vehicle_type`, migration `0007_resources`) is preceded by a `DO $$ … RAISE EXCEPTION $$` block that fails the migration while any row still holds a value, and the rendering test pins the guard's place before the drop.
- Derived rows (list columns, KPI tiles, matched containers, a scheme's scheduled, effective and expired readings and its Attention, which boundary of an area is in force on a day, a route's progress and its next stop, a session's open, ended, paused and stale) are computed, never persisted; `?validOn=` is how a period is read against a day, and no day is implied, since a default would need a timezone nobody stated.
- Ids are UUIDv7, generated by the client for anything a driver can create offline.
- A device-minted command id is a ledger's primary key. `driver_command` (Issue #104, ADR-0004) is the receipt of every command a driver's device ever sent, applied or rejected, and its `id` is the device's own UUIDv7 — the idempotency key the applier reads first, and the id of the row the command made (a session, a proof, an unload) in that row's own table, so one command is one id across the tables that say what was said and what happened. A command whose id the driver already sent is a replay: the first outcome answered again, with the row it made read back or the problem it was refused with, and nothing written — a 200, never a 409, because the key is the command and the command has already happened; two uploads of one batch overlapping meet that primary key (23505 `driver_command_pkey`), the savepoint rolls the loser's rows back and the first receipt is read and answered. The consequence: a key held by a row the caller cannot see — a command id another device minted, whatever its company — is answered as that command's rejection (409) but cannot be recorded, since the receipt's own key is that id, and goes to the log instead; it is the one rejection no receipt holds. A rejected command is otherwise kept whole, its body verbatim and the problem it was refused with as `jsonb`, for the reader (Resolution) that turns it into a Ticket.
- A ledger row's reference may be null where the rejection it records named nothing the writer reaches. `driver_command.route_id` is nullable: a command refused because no such route is assigned to the driver — another company's, another project's, or none — has no route the composite key could check, so the receipt is written with `route_id` null in the driver's own project, the device's claimed id kept in `body` beside the body it sent, and `driver_command_route_shape` holds such a row to a rejection naming no session and no pickup; `project_id` stays `NOT NULL`, so the row is the project's like every Execution row and the driver key stays checked. A route of the driver's project assigned to somebody else is named by its receipt and nothing else is read of it.
- A session is per Route, not a shift, and the assignment is two singular states. A `session` runs from `start-route` to `end-route` on one route, one device, one driver, its id the start command's; open, ended and paused are readings of `ended_at` and `paused_at`, so there is no status column. The Planned Assignment and the Actual Assignment are columns on `route`, the second written once by the session that started it and never by a form; the history of reassignment is the outbox's `route-reassigned` events, written when the driver or the vehicle moved, and not a ledger of its own.
- A rule the API holds under a lock may still be held by the database at the write, and then it is the one command's answer. `session_route_open_idx` and `session_driver_open_idx` are partial unique indexes over the rows with `ended_at` null — one live session per route, one per driver — and the API's backstop: `decide` judges the rule a statement earlier under the route's row lock, and where two of one driver's routes are started at once, each under its own lock, the loser's insert meets the index once the winner commits, its savepoint rolls back, the winner is read, and the command is recorded as the rejection the rule would have made ("Mads Jensen is already on route RC-1039; end it first"), the rest of its batch standing — a 23505 on a partial unique index turned into a 409 for one command, never the batch's, and noted in the log so that the write, and not the rule, can be seen to have held it.
- The outbox is one table and one writer. Every office command that changes what another context would hear about, every applied driver command whose decision names an event, and every rejected one (`command-rejected`, the receipt as payload) inserts its `outbox_event` rows through `emit`, in the request's transaction and after the rows they describe, so the event commits with the change or not at all; the payload is the wire resource as the route would answer it at that instant (a `Route`, a `Pickup` with the proof the command made, an `Unload`, the receipt), `occurred_at` the command's instant and not the request's, and `aggregate_id` a soft uuid on purpose, since an outbox row outlives nothing and joins nothing. It spreads `timestamps` and is not a ledger, because the relay will stamp `published_at`; its partial index over `id` alone where `published_at is null` is the second index without the tenant after the hook's e-mail index, for the same reason: the relay's read is one statement in the system that crosses companies. Nothing in part A reads the table back.
- The sync service has a role of its own and a publication over exactly what it syncs. `wms_sync` carries `REPLICATION` to open a logical replication slot, `BYPASSRLS` because forced row-level security would filter the initial snapshot for a role without it and the service reads every tenant by construction, `USAGE` on `wms` and one `GRANT SELECT` per synced table, and nothing else — it owns nothing and may write nothing; `NOLOGIN` until `pnpm db:bootstrap` gives it a login from `SYNC_DATABASE_URL`, which an environment without a PowerSync instance leaves unset. `CREATE PUBLICATION powersync FOR TABLE …` runs over exactly the tables the sync rules read (`SYNCED_TABLES` in `packages/db/src/sql/publication.ts`, the one spelling the migration is copied from and the rendering test holds it to; `user_account` is among them because every parameter query joins it to resolve the driver from the token's subject), so a table the rules do not name is never replicated; a table joins the rules by joining that list first, its grant and its `ALTER PUBLICATION … ADD TABLE` going into the file that adds it. The fence a device gets is the sync rules' column lists, not the database's policies.
- A display number is a per-company counter, not a hash and not a sequence. `company.next_route_number` (default 1000, so a demo number does not read as a count) is the next `RC-<n>` the company mints; generation takes a block in one `update … set next_route_number = next_route_number + N … returning`, whose row lock is the serialisation, so two runs never share a number and no route is ever renumbered. The prefix is presentation, spelled once in the contracts; the column stores the integer. `company.next_ticket_number` (Issue #109) is the second, on the same rule, taken one number per ticket and not in a block, since a ticket is one create and the lock is held for the rest of the request.
- A ticket's provenance is the outbox event's id: a soft uuid, and the consumer's idempotency key. `ticket.source_event_id` (Issue #109) names the `outbox_event` row the consumer made the ticket from and is not a key into it, for the reason `aggregate_id` is not: an outbox row outlives nothing and joins nothing, and a key from here would make it join something and never be pruned. `ticket_origin_shape` ties it to `created_by` — a ticket is a person's or an event's, never both and never neither. It is held by a partial unique index, `ticket_source_event_id_idx` over `(company_id, source_event_id) where source_event_id is not null`, so an event delivered twice is one ticket and the second attempt a 23505 the consumer reads back; `ticket_event.source_event_id` under `ticket_event_source_event_id_idx` is the second such key, for a `command-rejected` folded into the driver's open case as a comment. The consumer's contract is at-least-once from the queue and exactly one row through the index.
- A case's history is a ledger of kinds with shapes, and the case's row is a snapshot the ledger repeats. Every write to `ticket`'s row is a command — the create and seven commands, never a form save of the whole record — and every command appends one `ticket_event` in the same transaction through the one function that writes the row (`appendTicketEvent`, the Vehicle Allocation's `appendEvent` rule), so the history is complete by construction; a `PATCH` of the case's own fields appends nothing, since the history of a field edit is the audit log's (ADR-0005), and a comment is a row of the ledger that touches the case not at all. Each row carries the status and the assignee after the event, so the history reads without the row, and `ticket_event_kind_shape` is one `CASE` over the kind built from the domain's `TICKET_EVENT_SHAPES` (the proof ledger's precedent): a `created` row says nothing but the snapshot, a `status-changed` row carries a resolution exactly when its status is `completed`, a `comment` carries a body, may carry an attachment's key and may be the customer's to read. An Alert, which owns no resolution work, is current state and no ledger: its stamps are columns held to its status.
- The consumer opens and never closes. `ticketFor` (`@waste/domain/resolution/from-event`) answers a ticket for a `pickup-failed`, a `pickup-skipped` under a closing reason (`route-ended`, `route-cancelled`), a `pickup-problem-reported` and a `command-rejected`, and nothing for a driver's own skip, a dispatcher's removal, a regeneration, the route's own events, the completions, the corrections and the unloads — a ticket is made where nobody decided about the address, and a `route-cancelled` makes nothing of its own since its closed stops each arrive as `pickup-skipped`. A person closes: a later `pickup-corrected` is a comment for the adapter to show and not a completion. Resolution writes no row of Execution's, Planning's, Resources' or the Registry's: a re-collection is a Route the generation worker or the office makes, or a stop on one, and the ticket names it (`recollection_route_id`, with `resolution = recollected`, on a route that has not ended); a wrong outcome is corrected through Execution's own door and the ticket notes it in a comment.
- A Storage object's key is three UUIDs and a format, and the API never proxies bytes. `objectKeyPattern(extensions)` in `@waste/contracts/execution` is the one spelling of the path — `<companyId>/<routeId>/<commandId>.<ext>` for a proof's photo or signature, `<companyId>/<ticketId>/<objectId>.<ext>` for a ticket's attachment — where `<objectId>` is a UUID the client mints for the object before uploading it: a server-minted id (the comment's event id, as Issue #109 first specified it) is one no client could ever post, so the only reachable answer would have been the refusal. The contracts hold a key to the shape and to the bucket's formats (a proof's four image formats; a ticket's those and `pdf`, so a key of the one bucket never parses as a key of the other), the API holds the first two segments to the row's own company and route or ticket (400 at the field), and the bucket's policy per company prefix is Storage's.
- Money is an integer in minor units, with one rounding spelled twice and held together. Every amount (Issue #112) is an `integer` on a row, a line and an event or a `bigint` on a document's and a settlement's totals, in the currency's minor unit — øre for DKK — `_minor` in the column's name and never a decimal, so nothing is re-rounded downstream and two integers add up to what they say. VAT rounds half away from zero, per line, as Postgres's `round(numeric)` rounds and as Danish invoices show: `vatOf` in `@waste/domain/finance/money` (the sign taken off, the magnitude rounded, the sign put back, since `Math.round(-0.5)` is `-0` where Postgres says `-1`), `vat_minor = round(net_minor * vat_percent / 100.0)` in `billable_event_vat_shape` and `invoice_line_vat_shape`, and a database test writes rows the domain computed over odd amounts of both signs so the two cannot drift. The rate is the product's (`product.vat_percent`, whole percent, zero exempt, a product without one blocking its events with `no-vat-rate`), frozen on the event with the price; a price row or a rate that changes afterwards does not move an event already priced, which is what the customer will be told.
- A document is a ledger whose corrections are further documents. An Invoice is appended and never rewritten — "invoice number and original issued content are immutable" — so `invoice` and `invoice_line` spread `recorded` and their file revokes `UPDATE` and `DELETE` from the API role; a correction is a credit note, itself an Invoice of `kind = credit-note` naming the invoice it credits, from the same series under its own prefix, by quantity and never by amount, at most what remains of each line; a corrected pickup that was already invoiced becomes a `reversal` Billable Event the next run puts on the payer's next invoice as a negative line, so a payer whose period holds reversals alone is invoiced a negative net; and a wrong Unload is corrected by a new Unload row a `weight_review` names. "Sent" is the export's own row and never the invoice's. The number is the per-company counter's third series (`company.next_invoice_number`), taken one document at a time under the company's row lock and held for the rest of the transaction, so a run of a thousand invoices numbers them unbroken and a run that fails rolls its numbers back with its rows.
- A status is a reading where an event's stamps and its invoice line say it. A Billable Event is current state with no status column: `blocked` is `block_reason is not null`, `cancelled` is `cancelled_at is not null`, `invoiced` is an `invoice_line` naming it, `reversed` is a `reversal` event naming it, and `ready` is none of those (`billableEventStatus`, the later fact winning where two hold), read on every row through two `LATERAL` probes and stored nowhere, since two things move on the row after it is written — a reprice, a cancellation — and a stored status would be a fifth way of saying what the columns say. An Unload's review status is likewise its latest `weight_review` row's decision, or `captured` when there is none (`weightReviewOf`, the `assetStateOf` shape), and an invoice's credit standing is the sum of its lines' credits against their quantities.
- A provider's reach is one predicate over the assignment valid on the operating date, spelled once. `servesRoute` (`apps/api/src/auth/provider.ts`) is ADR-0001's sentence as SQL over a `route` row — `exists` one Service Area Assignment of the account's provider, valid on the route's `operating_date`, whose Service Area is valid on it too and names the route's scheme's planning area — and a SQL `false` for an account without a provider, so an office account never widens through it; the day is the operating date, the day the work is done, which is how the ADR's "service date" is read. It is built and proved and fences nothing yet; each family adds it as a provider account's second fence in an issue of its own, `inProjects` for the office and this for the provider, the way `assignedTo` is the driver's. What a provider's account reaches today is its own — its assignments, the areas they name, the prices under them and the settlements over them (`reachesAssignments`, `reachesThroughAssignments`) — and never a customer's price list, a billable event, a billing run or an invoice.
- A ledger row is never row-locked; a command over one locks its parent. `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` asks `UPDATE` privilege of the table, which `appendOnly` revoked from `wms_api` (42501), so a rule the API holds over a ledger row is held under the lock of the `timestamps` row it hangs off: a credit note under the billing run that issued the invoice, a weight review under the unload's route. Coarser than the row, which a correction can bear; and the ledger row itself never changes, so reading it before the lock is safe.
