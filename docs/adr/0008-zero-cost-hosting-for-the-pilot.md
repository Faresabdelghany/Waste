# Zero-cost hosting for the Pilot

The Pilot runs at no cost and with no payment card anywhere (#116's rule), on Supabase's Free plan for the database and Auth and on a host outside it for the API and the pg-boss worker. ADR-0007 chose two Fly Machines for the two processes; Fly asks for a card, and so, at the first service, did Render, the host the runtime research (#117) had ranked first on its documentation. Koyeb, the card-free alternative, is closed to new users. The research of 2026-09-29 (#134) went through every container host, tunnel and serverless platform against primary sources; one host ran the image's Dockerfile unchanged, always on, without a card, at the time of writing: **Suga** (Suga Cloud, Nitric Pty Ltd), a free tier of 0.1 vCPU and 256 MiB in `europe-west1` (Belgium), a stable `*.suga.run` hostname, no documented idle spin-down, and outbound capped at 5 Mbit/s per replica. The prototype of #134 was deployed there and measured; this record is what those measurements decided, and #149 is the implementation.

## Decision

**Suga Free hosts the Pilot's API and worker as one web service running two processes under a small supervisor** — the worker shape #128 decided, unchanged: `apps/api` and `apps/worker` keep their composition roots, probes and shutdowns; only the API's port is public; the supervisor (`apps/pilot/src/supervise.ts`) restarts the worker child with backoff (5 s doubling to 5 min, reset after 10 min up) while the API runs, exits with the API's code when the API exits, and forwards SIGTERM to both children and waits for both, killing one still up after 20 s. The worker's probe port stays on loopback inside the container.

**Suga builds the image itself from `apps/pilot/Dockerfile` on the deploy branch `pilot`, at every push**, with the repository root as context, through its GitHub app's read-only access to this repository. There is no registry image (a free organisation may not deploy one), no deploy hook, and no keep-alive, since the container is always on. A push is a build (18–26 s) and a rollout that starts the new container the instant it SIGTERMs the old one; the downtime is the new container's boot. **`pilot` moves only through the protected `release` of `pilot-database.yml`**, after the migrations and the fingerprint pass (#133's door), so a code change that needs a migration can never go live before its migration; `main` is never what Suga tracks. Because Suga passes no commit id into its builds (its build arguments are values typed into the service's form), the release does not fast-forward `pilot` to `main`'s commit but writes one release commit on it: `main`'s tree plus `apps/pilot/release.json` naming the released commit, with the branch's head and the released commit as parents (a fast-forward by construction, `main`'s history in its ancestry). The Dockerfile writes that file into `build.json` wherever it is present (and CI's `SOURCE_COMMIT` otherwise), so `GET /healthz` on the Pilot names the released commit of `main`, and the release's proof stays what #152 set: three consecutive observations of `/healthz` naming the released commit and `/readyz` 200, the old build's answers during Suga's build and rollout counting as "not yet". Before the migrations, the release holds the commit to CI's proof — its verify and pilot-image check runs completed with success — which is what the resolved image proved in the door's old second step.

**The image bundles the two apps with esbuild** (`apps/pilot/src/bundle.ts`, one bundle per entry, workspace TypeScript and npm dependencies inlined, Node built-ins native), and the supervisor runs plain `node` on the bundles. Measured on Suga against tsx at runtime: the API listening in 13.2 s and the worker running its queues in 18.2 s, where tsx took 100–145 s at a tenth of a core; a redeploy's downtime 14 s, where it was 2.5–3 minutes; the cgroup at 72 MiB idle and 86–91 MiB under ten clients, of a hard 256 MiB, where tsx idled at 135–166 MiB and peaked at 172. No line of either app changes for it.

**Supabase's root CA is trusted through `NODE_EXTRA_CA_CERTS`.** node-postgres, pg-boss's driver, reads `sslmode=require` as verify-full and refused the session pooler's chain ("self-signed certificate in certificate chain") until the published root, `prod-ca-2021.crt` (SHA-256 fingerprint `80:70:25:AD:50:D4:ED:21:9D:2C:9C:7D:29:9C:00:4F:82:4E:B0:0C:F7:F6:5A:FE:F6:07:D0:7B:72:E6:CA:FA`, valid to 2031-04-26), was in the image and named by that variable. With it both drivers verify the pooler; without it the API connected only because postgres.js does not verify on `require`.

**The worker runs at variant C**: `WORKER_POLLING_INTERVAL_SECONDS=30` (#128's poll, so an on-demand generation starts inside a minute; every queue's poll, the relay's successor delay and pg-boss's cron and flow intervals are raised to it and never lowered, and no `schedule` cron is touched), pg-boss's supervise and queue-cache intervals at 300 s (`WORKER_SUPERVISE_INTERVAL_SECONDS`, `WORKER_QUEUE_CACHE_INTERVAL_SECONDS`), and **pg-boss's pool idle timeout at or above the longest interval that uses the pool** (`WORKER_BOSS_IDLE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS=600`, or 0 for never). Most of the idle egress had been pg-boss reconnecting every poll through pg-pool's default 10 s idle timeout, a TLS handshake and a startup through the pooler each time. Supabase's own meter (`egress_supavisor`) over variant C's clean hours reads 1.65–1.67 MB an hour, **1.20–1.22 GB a 30.4-day month, 24% of the Free plan's 5 GB**, leaving about 3.8 GB for the office and the drivers; at pg-boss's defaults the same worker metered 2.42 GB (48%). The binding cap is Supabase's, not the host's, which documents no monthly bandwidth figure. Every knob is read once in each app's `env.ts`, and absent means the code's own default: the Pilot's values are Suga's environment, listed in `apps/pilot/README.md`, never a default in the code.

**The pools stay inside the pooler's 15 per role.** The Pilot's ceilings are 10 on `wms_api` (the API's request pool 5 and probe 1, the worker's API-role pool 3 and probe 1) and 5 on `wms_worker` (pg-boss 3 and the worker's pool 2) — `DATABASE_POOL_MAX=5`, `WORKER_API_POOL_MAX=3`, `WORKER_POOL_MAX=2` — against Supabase Nano's pool size of 15 per user and database in session mode, its 200 client connections, and Postgres's 60 in all, of which 19–20 were in use by Supabase's own services. Unauthenticated traffic showed 1–2 and 4.

**Gate B restores ADR-0007.** The two-host deployment on Fly stays the target and is not rewritten; ADR-0007 is `status: deferred` with one sentence pointing here, and `docs/README.md`'s list of statuses carries `deferred`. This record is a Pilot hosting deviation, never a replacement architecture: the day the Pilot has a budget, the same image's two closures run on two Machines with nothing in either app changed.

## Considered options

- **Render Free, with a card.** #117's first choice and the shape's original host: card-free on its documentation, a card at the first service in practice (a 402 from the API, the same prompt in the dashboard). With a card the 5 GB egress month becomes a bill instead of a suspension, and the 15-minute idle spin-down would have needed the keep-alive. Rejected by the no-card rule.
- **Koyeb Free** for the worker, #128's fallback split. Closed to new users since its acquisition; the free plan is being removed. Not available.
- **HostStack.** Card-free, 256 MB, Dockerfile builds; but it sleeps after 60 minutes without traffic unless a card is on file, which freezes the worker, and its acceptable-use policy forbids circumventing quotas, so a keep-alive needs its word first. Second in line if Suga ends.
- **Self-hosting behind a tunnel** (a Cloudflare Worker over Workers VPC, Tailscale Funnel, zrok). Needs a dedicated, always-on machine; the tunnels each carry a term or an interstitial. Third in line.
- **Serverless.** The API fits Vercel Hobby behind a thin entry, under non-commercial terms and CPU-hour caps that pause the whole team; the worker fits nowhere as it is, and a drain-and-exit worker ticked by `pg_cron` was seen pausing a Hobby team. GitHub Actions as the worker's runtime is out by its terms ("don't use Actions … as part of a serverless application"), not only by its five-minute floor and 60-day auto-disable. Rejected.
- **One merged Node process** instead of the supervisor: a new composition root and cross-app imports for a host constraint that Gate B removes. Rejected, as in #128.
- **A registry image and a deploy hook**, #128's delivery path: not offered on Suga's free tier, and not needed, since Suga builds from the repository on every push. The GHCR half of gate 5 is proven (a public repository's package pulls anonymously) and kept in reserve for a host that pulls images.
- **Suga tracking `main`**, so every merge deploys. Rejected: a merge whose migration has not yet been released would run against a database behind it, which #133's door exists to prevent. The deploy branch costs one release commit per release and nothing else.
- **A plain fast-forward of `pilot` to `main`'s commit**, with the commit passed as a build argument. Not possible on Suga, whose build arguments are static; a commit read from `.git` in the build context depends on how the host's builder checks out, which is not documented. The release file in the tree is what the host builds by construction.

## Consequences

- `apps/pilot` (`@waste/pilot`) carries the supervisor, the esbuild bundle step, the CA file and `NODE_EXTRA_CA_CERTS`, and the memory line (`PILOT_MEMORY_LOG_SECONDS`: the cgroup's current, peak and limit beside each process's RSS, off unless set), in the turbo gates. The knobs and the pool ceilings live in the two apps' `env.ts` as optional values. CI builds and smoke-tests the image on every run against a database nobody answers on; it publishes nothing and calls no hook.
- The release's Deploy step is the release commit on `pilot` under the job's token (`contents: write` on the release job alone); if `pilot` is protected by a ruleset, the workflow needs a bypass, the owner's to grant. `.github/workflows/keep-alive.yml` is not written: nothing spins down, and Actions' terms would not cover it in any case.
- The Suga service, its environment (the three secrets and the knobs above) and the `pilot` branch's selection are the owner's hand steps, listed on #149 and in `apps/pilot/README.md`.
- The accepted risks. Suga's free tier shrank once this year (0.5 vCPU and 1 GiB in July, 0.1 vCPU and 256 MiB now) and its agreement lets it end "at any time, with or without notice"; a hard 256 MiB with about 165 MiB in hand today; **unasked container moves**, three in forty minutes on the first day (09:12, 09:23, 09:42Z, "Stopping previous version … Server assigned"), each about 14 s of downtime with the bundle; an exited container restarted on a growing backoff (4, 15, 24, 45 s). With no card anywhere nothing can bill: over Supabase's 5 GB the project meets a notice and a grace period, then its fair-use policy, and on Suga the 5 Mbit/s is the ceiling. The order of trials if Suga ends is HostStack, then a tunnel.
- What the office sees: the API at its `*.suga.run` hostname, which the web on Vercel calls; a deploy is a release and about a minute; a redeploy or a move is 14 s of 503 from Suga's edge. Whether a polling worker counts as activity for Supabase's weekly pause (#130) is still unverified; the one-click restore is the fallback.
- Every figure above is a measurement of one day (2026-09-29) on one replica and is re-read in the first week: memory drift (67 MiB after boot to 76 MiB after two hours, watched), the moves, and the meter.

## Owed, as #149's acceptance

Two measurements the prototype could not take are read on the merged image and written on #149 before this record moves to `accepted`:

1. **The authenticated readings.** Memory under authenticated traffic and under a generation run on the host, and gate 3's connection count with the API's request pool in use. They waited on a token; since #150 a password sign-in gives one.
2. **A day's count of Suga's moves** without traffic, from the merged image on variant C, and whether anything idles the service when nobody calls it; with it, the memory line and Supabase's egress meter read once after that day.
